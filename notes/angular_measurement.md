# Angular measurement of the saved wave/ray discrepancy

## Question and result

This short extension asks whether the difference between the **two fixed simulated optical models** survives angular integration and ideal photon shot noise. It does not ask which real textile is present, whether a person notices a difference, or whether a metasurface beats conventional imaging. No optical simulation was rerun. Dyeing remains outside the project.

In the centered Ez Gaussian-beam case, the original models have nearly the same backward total: wave R = 0.26691930435 and ray R = 0.26880000000. With an expected 100 incident photons, the total-power-only ideal likelihood-ratio test has 49.45% balanced error. Partitioning the same backward hemisphere into 90 nonoverlapping 2-degree sectors gives 6.38% error. Removing total brightness through a nuisance-gain profile gives 6.39% error at 90 sectors. The underlying angular difference can therefore survive this **idealized measurement model**, even when integrated reflection is similar.

This quantifies one reason an angle-resolved observation can be useful. It does not establish a benefit relative to a specific high-resolution RGB camera, multi-light RGB capture, detector design, or metaoptic. Spatial resolution was not varied here.

## Inputs and scope

The only optical inputs are `data/fiber_wave_gaussian_beam.npz` and `data/fiber_wave_gaussian_beam_metrics.json`, generated by `textile_sim/fiber_wave_beam.py`. The associated derivation and validation are in `notes/fiber_wave_beam.md`.

- Seven parallel circular cylinders, radius 5 um, gap 0.4 um, synthetic lossless index 1.55.
- Wavelength 0.55 um. Separate Ez and Hz polarization experiments; their photon budgets are not pooled.
- Coherent Gaussian beam, 1/e amplitude radius 10 um; offsets 0, 5, and 10 um.
- The wave model has finite angular divergence; the ray model uses parallel incident rays with the matched Gaussian intensity profile. This remaining optical-model difference is inherited, not resolved by this analysis.
- The saved wave intensity has 8,640 uniform angular samples. The saved ray distribution has 180 histogram bins, each 2 degrees wide, from 250,000 lossless Fresnel-roulette trajectories per case.

These are **2-D transverse angular sectors** of an infinite-cylinder calculation, not 3-D detector solid angles. An actual finite-length fiber bundle, imaging lens, numerical aperture, angular blur, polarization optics, detector efficiency, and background would require a new forward measurement model.

## Common angular integration without power renormalization

The measured backward interval is theta in [pi/2, 3*pi/2), measured from the incident +x direction. The original negative angles are unwrapped into [pi, 3*pi/2). The common fine layout has 90 adjacent 2-degree bins. Coarser layouts merge those bins into 1, 6, or 18 sectors, with widths 180, 30, or 10 degrees. Every incident photon can enter at most one detected bin. No channel duplication or extra illumination is introduced by using more sectors.

For model h and sector j:

\[
p_{h,j}=\int_{\Theta_j} f_h(\theta)\,d\theta,
\qquad \sum_j p_{h,j}=R_h.
\]

The wave density is scattered power divided by independently normalized incident Gaussian-beam power. Only the backward part is used. Its forward scattered power alone is **not** transmitted power because the forward field also contains incident/scattered interference.

Ray probabilities are exact sums of the saved histogram masses. We make no statement about angles finer than those original 2-degree bins. For the wave, simply adding 48 midpoint samples per 2-degree bin leaves a small quadrature error: as much as 4.44e-7 absolute in total R. Instead, the periodic real Fourier interpolant of the saved samples is integrated analytically over each common sector:

\[
f(\theta)\simeq\sum_m c_m e^{im\theta},\qquad
\int_a^b f\,d\theta
=c_0(b-a)+\sum_{m\ne0}c_m\frac{e^{imb}-e^{ima}}{im}.
\]

The code uses conjugate symmetry and treats the even-grid Nyquist mode as a real cosine. The integral is exact for the interpolant; adequacy for the optical function is checked by independently rebuilding the interpolant from every second, fourth, and eighth original sample (4,320, 2,160, and 1,080 points). These are genuine subsampled grids, not averaged copies whose integrals agree by construction.

Observed validation over all six cases:

- Maximum per-bin difference between original and subsampled Fourier integrals: **2.31e-16 absolute probability**.
- Maximum difference of integrated wave R from the independent saved GL512 backward quadrature: **6.94e-15**.
- Ray integrated totals agree with saved R to floating-point precision.
- All wave and ray bin probabilities are positive. The least populated ray bin contains **39 trajectories**; no zero-probability smoothing was needed.

Neither model is renormalized to match the other's R. Subsequent normalized angular profiles appear only inside the explicitly declared nuisance-gain statistic.

## Photon model, likelihoods, and exact information decomposition

Let N be the known **expected** number of incident photons in one polarization experiment. Assume Poisson illumination, unit efficiency, full backward acceptance, no detector noise other than shot noise, and known bin boundaries. Independent thinning then gives

\[
Y_j\sim\operatorname{Pois}(N p_{h,j}).
\]

An exactly fixed realized number of incident photons would yield a multinomial experiment including the undetected category; that is a different model. The present independent-Poisson model is stated explicitly and used consistently in both formulas and random trials.

For wave versus ray, the known-N log likelihood ratio is

\[
\ell_{\rm known}
=\sum_jY_j\log\frac{p_{w,j}}{p_{r,j}}
-N(R_w-R_r).
\]

When wave is true, its expectation is the directed Poisson KL divergence:

\[
D_{w\parallel r}
=N\sum_j\left[p_{w,j}\log\frac{p_{w,j}}{p_{r,j}}
+p_{r,j}-p_{w,j}\right].
\]

When ray is true, the expected score is minus the reverse KL divergence. Both directions and 2D (expected/Asimov deviance) are saved. Expected log likelihood is **not an error probability, a human threshold, or a generic significance in standard deviations**.

Write q_h,j = p_h,j/R_h. The wave-true information separates exactly:

\[
\frac{D_{w\parallel r}}N
=\underbrace{R_w\log\frac{R_w}{R_r}+R_r-R_w}_{\text{total reflected flux}}
+\underbrace{R_w\,D_{\rm KL}(q_w\parallel q_r)}_{\text{conditional angular shape}}.
\]

If a common multiplicative illumination/detection gain g is unknown, independently profiling it under each model gives

\[
\hat g_h=\frac{\sum_jY_j}{N R_h},\qquad
\ell_{\rm profile}=\sum_jY_j\log\frac{q_{w,j}}{q_{r,j}}.
\]

This equals the conditional multinomial score at the observed reflected count. Its wave-true expectation is N R_w KL(q_w||q_r). It removes total-flux evidence. With one sector it is identically zero; with zero detected photons it also gives a tie. The physical count-generating distributions still use their original R values. The profile-gain decision is a generalized/conditional likelihood-ratio rule, not a claim of Bayes optimality for an unspecified prior over gain.

In this ideal setup, finer nested angular bins cannot reduce information: a coarse observation can be recovered by summing fine counts. That monotonicity follows from data processing and is checked in tests. The useful numerical question is **how much information survives a specified bin width and photon budget**. The calculation does not choose an optimal detector resolution because per-channel hardware costs, blur, and read/dark noise are absent.

## Independent Poisson Monte Carlo

For two specified conditions only (Ez/0 um and Hz/10 um), generate 40,000 independent count vectors under wave truth and 40,000 under ray truth. N = 100, fixed seeds 20261012 and 20261017. Classify by the sign of the score; exact ties use independent fair coins. The table gives the equal-weight average of the two directional errors. Each directional error and its 95% Wilson interval are in the JSON.

| Condition / score | 1 sector | 6 sectors | 18 sectors | 90 sectors |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Ez/0, known N | 49.45% | 40.97% | 26.97% | 6.38% |
| Ez/0, profile gain | 49.87% | 41.05% | 26.92% | 6.39% |

The one-sector profile statistic has exactly 50% expected error; its finite-trial numbers fluctuate around that value. All sampled mean log likelihood ratios agree with their independently calculated expectations within **1.90 Monte Carlo standard errors**. These classification trials assume the saved optical dictionaries are fixed. They do not include uncertainty in optical model parameters or unknown actual material properties.

## Ray-dictionary sampling uncertainty and the high-N limit

Physical photon shot noise and Monte Carlo optical-simulation noise are different. In this lossless saved ray experiment, trajectories have weight one and each trajectory finishes in one angular category. The probability estimate has multinomial covariance

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(\hat p_i,\hat p_j)
=\frac{p_i\delta_{ij}-p_i p_j}{M},\qquad M=250000.
\]

The ratio of uncertainty in the predicted photon mean to a channel's physical shot-noise standard deviation is

\[
\frac{N\sqrt{p_j(1-p_j)/M}}{\sqrt{N p_j}}
=\sqrt{\frac{N(1-p_j)}{M}}.
\]

Its maximum in this analysis is approximately 0.020 at N = 100, 0.063 at N = 1,000, and **0.200 at N = 10,000**. At N = 10,000 the associated per-channel variance can therefore approach 4% of physical shot-noise variance. The cumulative effect across sectors also matters; these point estimates must not be extrapolated to arbitrarily large N as proof of arbitrarily precise physical model separation.

For an additional dictionary sensitivity check, 400 plug-in multinomial bootstrap dictionaries per optical case are generated, keeping a forward/undetected category with probability 1-R_ray. Each bootstrap is propagated to the directed information scores. This includes angular-bin anticorrelation and total-R uncertainty rather than independently jittering bins. It is a sampling-sensitivity calculation, not a posterior or an experimental confidence interval for the actual textile.

At centered Ez and N = 100, the bootstrap 2.5–97.5 percentile range is [4.256, 4.484] nats for 90-sector known-N information, compared with the nominal 4.354 nats. For the one-sector flux information, it is [0.0000227, 0.002520] nats, confirming that the tiny brightness difference is relatively sensitive to ray dictionary noise. At Hz/10 um, the corresponding 90-sector range is [3.024, 3.270] nats around 3.126. There is substantial angular information in these fixed synthetic examples even after acknowledging finite ray statistics, but the simulation is not an empirical measurement.

## Implementation and checks

Main module: `textile_sim/angular_measurement.py`. Functions include analytic periodic-bin integration, exact histogram aggregation, directed Poisson information/decomposition, known-N and profiled-gain scores, dictionary bootstrap, and independent Poisson trials.

Ten unit tests pass. The checks cover:

- analytic Fourier polynomial integrals on shifted odd/even grids, including the Nyquist cosine;
- histogram angle ordering and power-preserving sector aggregation;
- rejection of malformed angular grids;
- Poisson KL checked against an explicit expectation of independent `scipy.stats.poisson.logpmf` values;
- exact flux/shape decomposition and zero shape evidence for proportional profiles;
- information monotonicity under genuine nested coarsening;
- profile score checked against separately maximized Poisson likelihoods;
- photon-budget scaling, zero support, and probability validation;
- all six saved optical distributions checked against independent total-R references and subsampled Fourier quadrature.

The random-count experiments additionally check expected likelihoods using separately generated Poisson observations. Figures were visually inspected for axes, channel labels, units, and the stated ideal-model scope.

An independent cross-module review by the ray-simulation agent confirmed the Poisson KL decomposition, gain profiling, preservation of the true reflected totals, Fourier phase origin/Nyquist handling, and the absence of any extra cosine factor for the already normalized angular density. It also checked the source ray implementation: incident positions are iid Gaussian draws, Fresnel choices use roulette, absorption is zero, all six saved cases have zero residual/failed rays, and ray weights are one. These source conditions justify the multinomial dictionary bootstrap. Integer-looking histogram bins alone would not be sufficient evidence for arbitrary weighted or stratified ray data.

Reproduce from the project directory:

```bash
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 python -m textile_sim.angular_measurement
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 python -m unittest discover -s tests -p 'test_angular_measurement.py' -v
```

- `data/angular_measurement_metrics.json`: all information scores, convergence, dictionary bootstrap, Monte Carlo errors, intervals, seeds, and runtime.
- `data/angular_measurement_information.png`: angular-channel and photon-budget dependence.
- `data/angular_measurement_errors.png`: independent Poisson classification errors for the two tested conditions.
